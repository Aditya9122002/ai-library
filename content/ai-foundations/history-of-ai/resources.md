Our lesson gives you the core mental model. These resources go deeper — they add a clearer timeline, a historical review of the field’s boom-and-bust cycles, and a beginner-friendly watchlist for how AI moved from rules to machine learning to generative AI.

## Articles

**[The History of Artificial Intelligence](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/history-of-artificial-intelligence)**
A clean, readable timeline from IBM that connects early milestones, AI winters, machine learning, and the generative AI era. It complements the lesson by giving the learner a broader chronology without getting overly technical. 

**[Brief History of Artificial Intelligence](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33038991/)**
A short review article that summarizes major turning points in AI and explicitly calls out recurring AI winters and the need for realistic expectations. It works well here because it adds a more formal historical overview in a compact format. 

**[Ethics of Artificial Intelligence and Robotics](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2025/entries/ethics-ai/)**
Not a pure history article, but it gives a concise historical framing of the term “AI,” including the early decades, the AI winter, and the post-2010 expansion of the label. Use it when the learner wants a little more context on how the field’s identity changed over time. 

## Videos

**[What Is Artificial Intelligence? Crash Course AI #1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a0_lo_GDcFw)**
A beginner-friendly starting point from Crash Course that traces AI’s history while explaining what AI can and cannot do today. It matches this chapter well because it stays accessible without assuming technical background. 

**[Crash Course: Artificial Intelligence](https://www.pbs.org/show/crash-course-artificial-intelligence/)**
This series page is useful if a learner wants the full Crash Course AI sequence, not just one episode. PBS says the series teaches AI by tracing its history and examining how it is used today, which makes it a strong companion to this chapter.
